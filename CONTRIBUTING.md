# Contributing

Thank you for helping make games and interactive experiences easier for more people to play.

Inclusive Game Design welcomes contributions from players, developers, designers, researchers, educators, accessibility practitioners, and people who use assistive technologies.

## Good contributions

Useful contributions include:

- A clearly described barrier you encountered in a game or interactive experience
- A practical design or implementation pattern
- A small reference implementation
- A testing method that helps teams find barriers earlier
- Corrections or clarifications to existing guidance
- Stronger citations to standards, platform documentation, or research
- Examples for engines, frameworks, or platforms we do not yet cover

## How we write guidance

Please help us keep recommendations:

1. **Barrier focused.** Explain who can be excluded and how before proposing a solution.
2. **Practical.** Give designers or developers something they can actually try.
3. **Evidence aware.** Cite authoritative standards, platform guidance, or research when making general recommendations.
4. **Honest about scope.** Do not describe a technique as making a game "accessible" in a universal sense.
5. **Testable.** When possible, include a way to verify the behavior.
6. **Technology neutral first.** Explain the design principle before a framework-specific implementation.

## Examples

Reference implementations should be intentionally small. They should demonstrate one pattern without requiring someone to understand a complete Labyrinth Foundation project.

When contributing code, include the platform or framework version you tested and a short description of how you verified the example.

## Lived experience

Firsthand reports of accessibility barriers are valuable. Please describe the experience in your own terms. Contributors do not need to disclose a diagnosis, disability, or other personal information to explain a barrier.

## Sources

Prefer primary sources such as W3C/WAI, platform accessibility documentation, accessibility guidelines from game platforms, and peer reviewed research. Clearly distinguish a requirement in a standard from a recommendation, observation, or Labyrinth Foundation practice.

## Conduct

Be respectful when discussing accessibility needs, design tradeoffs, and other contributors' experiences. Assume that different players can encounter different barriers in the same design.

## Questions and proposals

For substantial new guidance, opening an issue first is encouraged so the scope, evidence, and testing approach can be discussed before a large contribution is written.
